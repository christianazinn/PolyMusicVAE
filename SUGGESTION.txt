My first thought for how the latent diffusion might work for arbitrary track-measure infilling was something like this: In the attached picture we have 5 instruments and 8 measures, with 6 track-measures selected for infilling. Hence there would be a 5x8 grid of latents on which we would perform diffusion, with each track-measure replaced with its latent. I think latents on the order of 128 dimensions might be appropriate for this. That gives us a (640, 8)-shaped latent space for this particular example, and we haven't yet told the model what instrument is on each track (that can be done by pre-pending a 1-hot vector to each track, so now the latent space is (640, 9)-shaped). In general, we can handle variable-length inputs and additional tracks up to pre-specified amounts by silence-padding. If we wanted to handle up to t tracks and n measures with a d-dimensional latent space, our diffusion model would need to operate on inputs of shape (t*d, n+1), and I don't know if that's reasonable for reasonable values of t and d. I have an idea to reduce the complexity, though - what if we focused only on single-track accompaniment generation and trained two different bar-wise VAE's - one to represent the context and the other to represent the instrument to be added? We could then simply stack those two representations and therefore have the diffusion model operate on inputs of shape (2*d, n+1). This scales fantastically well.
